STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE: MOTOR & MACHINERY PREVENTIVE MAINTENANCE & TROUBLESHOOTING
Industry: Automated Textile Manufacturing
Document ID: SOP-MNT-2026-04 (Rev. 2)

1. OBJECTIVE
   To establish a systematic preventive maintenance (PM) schedule, correct working methods, and a structured fault-checker guide for all electric motors, drives, and driven mechanical loads to minimize downtime and ensure safety.

2. SAFETY PRECAUTIONS & LOCKOUT/TAGOUT (LOTO)
- Before performing any maintenance on motors (e.g., [EQ-SPN-101], [EQ-WEV-201]), technicians MUST execute plant LOTO protocols.
- Verify zero energy state using a calibrated multimeter across all phases and to ground before touching terminal boxes.
- Ensure all rotating shafts have come to a complete physical standstill and kinetic energy has fully dissipated.

3. ROUTINE INSPECTION CHECKLISTS
3.1. Daily Checks (Operators & Shift Technicians)
- Monitor motor casing temperature using an infrared thermometer (Threshold: < 75°C under normal load).
- Listen for abnormal humming, bearing squeal, or mechanical vibration.
- Check cooling fins and air intake grilles for accumulation of cotton lint and dust; clear immediately.

3.2. Weekly Checks
- Inspect belt tensions on carding and spinning frames. Adjust tensioners to prevent slip without overloading motor shafts.
- Check VFD display panels for fault history codes, harmonic distortion warnings, or overcurrent spikes.
- Inspect flexible couplings and alignment indicators for signs of wear or angular misalignment.

3.3. Monthly Checks (Electrical & Mechanical Team)
- Insulation Resistance (Megger) Testing: Measure winding-to-ground resistance. Minimum acceptable value is $2\ \text{M}\Omega$ at $500\text{V DC}$.
- Vibration Analysis: Measure velocity and acceleration metrics on motor drive-end and non-drive-end bearings.
- Thermographic Scanning: Perform thermal imaging of electrical control panels and motor terminal connections.

4. CORRECT WORKING METHODS (BEST PRACTICES)
- Motor Alignment Method: When reinstalling a motor, use a laser alignment tool to keep angular misalignment below $0.05^\circ$ and parallel offset below $0.05\text{ mm}$ to prevent premature bearing failure.
- Correct Greasing Method: Clean the zerk fitting before grease injection. Apply measured shots using a calibrated grease gun (calculate exact grease quantity using formula: $G = 0.005 \times D \times B$, where $D$ is outer diameter and $B$ is bearing width). Rotate the shaft slowly during greasing to distribute evenly.

5. FAULT-CHECKER & TROUBLESHOOTING GUIDE

5.1. Fault Code: Motor Overheating (Casing Temp > 85°C)
- Step 1: Check current draw on all three phases using a clamp meter. Compare against the rated full-load amps ($FLA$) on the nameplate.
- Step 2: If current is balanced and below $FLA$, inspect cooling fins for lint blockage or ambient ventilation failure.
- Step 3: If current is unbalanced or high, check for mechanical binding in the driven load or voltage unbalance from the supply.

5.2. Fault Code: Excessive Vibration & Bearing Noise
- Step 1: Decouple the motor from the load and run it independently.
- Step 2: If vibration persists, inspect motor mounting bolts, baseplate rigidity, and bearing condition. Replace bearings if radial play exceeds tolerance.
- Step 3: If vibration disappears when decoupled, check for shaft misalignment, unbalance in the pulley/gear, or coupling wear.

5.3. Fault Code: VFD Overcurrent Trip (OC Fault)
- Step 1: Inspect output cables for short circuits or insulation breakdown to ground.
- Step 2: Check mechanical load for sudden jams, blockages, or over-torque conditions.
- Step 3: Review VFD acceleration/deceleration ramp times; increase ramp time if parameters are too aggressive for high-inertia loads like large spinning frames.

6. ESCALATION & RECORD KEEPING
- Log all maintenance activities, fault codes, readings, and component replacements into the Computerized Maintenance Management System (CMMS) immediately after task completion.
